# Active research lessons

Updated 15 November 2026. Read this file and `README.md` before research, refreshes and reviews. These are process requirements and hypotheses to evaluate prospectively, not fitted numerical weights or a promise of profitable selections. Preserve old decisions when a lesson changes.

## 1. Retrieve the evidence that can change the decision

The 14 November Portugal/Sweden report had official attempts and SOT, but no comparable xG. A later direct-provider retrieval supplied chance quality for both teams and their opponents. Portugal's away Norway win concealed a 1.04–2.52 xG disadvantage; Sweden's 43–46 attempt balance concealed a 6.74–2.37 xG advantage across four group games. Counts alone could mislead in either direction. The later data cannot be represented as evidence used in the original forecast; availability at the original cutoff is not established.

For a serious win/goal finalist, make a bounded attempt to retrieve comparable chance-quality data from a credible provider when the initial source omits it. Log unsuccessful access, provider definitions and remaining gaps. If the missing evidence could reverse the case, downgrade the decision. Do not invent xG from shots or average incompatible providers. For other markets, prioritize the corresponding event's distribution and opportunity rather than demanding irrelevant xG.

Evidence: [Nov14 review](../research/2026-11-14/post-match-review.md), [retrospective baseline](../research/2026-11-14/audit-evidence/both-fixtures-baseline-xg.json).

## 2. Conditions must resolve a real uncertainty

Portugal's absences, replacement defensive cover, Denmark's transition/set-piece routes and draw incentives existed before the loss. An acceptable XI would not automatically establish a win probability sufficient for 1.50. Explicitly ask: **if the expected XI starts and the quote stays unchanged, is the substantive case already strong enough?** If not, keep reserve/pass/insufficient evidence and name what more is needed. A conditional label must not conceal an unresolved football or price objection. Show final selections before research-only candidates.

Apply this in club football too: manager/system, opponent quality, availability and incentives change the relevance of historical samples. Do not assume internationals are the entire cause or domestic games restore an edge.

Evidence: [independent audit](../research/2026-11-14/method-challenge-review.md).

## 3. Test the exact market, including its failed observations

On 13 November Iceland won 5–0 but recorded one corner; the Netherlands won 7–0 but recorded five. Iceland 5+ and Netherlands 6+ corners both failed. Earlier Iceland evidence already included a 3–0 win with two corners. Dominance and goals do not establish corner volume. For corner finalists compare exact threshold hit/miss counts, distinct matches, venue/opponents, delivery and block patterns, and early-lead game states. A small extra price is not a reason to replace a better-supported result direction. Compare alternatives before recommending a switch; do not retroactively endorse the winning alternative.

Evidence: [verified Nov13 results](../research/2026-11-13/result-verification.json), [original market-choice audit](../research/2026-11-13/archive/friday-13-november-2026-market-choice-audit.md).

## 4. Player props need opportunities, roles and minutes

Cucurella recorded zero tackles on 12 November while Spain had 70% possession; only one ground duel was recorded for him. Spain already dominated before Czechia's late red card. Blocks, interceptions and clearances are not tackles. Require a credible route for repeated opponent attacks into the player's zone, with role/minutes and relevant comparable match counts. Do not equate a player's ability or tackle success percentage with sufficient attempts. Where direct matchup/carry data is missing, say so instead of inventing a winger assignment.

A player reaching a statistical line after coming on does not establish that a pre-match non-starter bet won. Verify exact starter, substitution and settlement rules; never multiply singles and call it an observed builder quote.

Evidence: [Nov12 audit](../research/2026-11-12/archive/thursday-12-november-2026-results-audit.md).

## 5. Audit both wins and losses symmetrically

Portugal's earlier Denmark away win had a 2.25–0.76 xG advantage: the data does not support calling every Portugal win lucky. Sweden won on 14 November with two penalties and only 0.70–1.14 non-penalty xG: a winning reserve does not prove the original price was good. Remove penalties, dismissals, favourable reverse fixtures and weak opponents consistently for both teams; distinguish sensitivity checks from independent observations. Contextual tactical reasoning should explain statistical relevance, not supply invented fixed weights for managers, keepers, referees or duels.

Keep user-reported tickets, research statuses, ordinary outcomes, promotion triggers and actual bookmaker settlements separate. Candidate losses remain visible even when no final selections were approved. Winning passes remain passes. Preserve historical BTTS No, but suggest **BTTS Yes only** going forward; a rejected No is not evidence for Yes.

Evidence: [Nov14 review](../research/2026-11-14/post-match-review.md), [earlier three-day audit](../research/methodology-audit-2026-11-14/audit.md).

## 6. Test improvements prospectively

For the next **four completed research slates**, including resumed domestic leagues where researched, review: coverage of the stated fixture universe; depth reached for finalists; missing-data retrieval attempts; explicit substantive versus matchday conditions; exact-market counterevidence; pre-kickoff status/price/XI capture; and whether every important pass and zero-selection slate was saved. Count failures as well as successes. This is a process review, not enough data to establish profitability or calibrate feature weights. Keep longer-run outcome/calibration evaluation separate, and evaluate forecasts only when genuine probabilities were recorded before kickoff.

For a 44-match day: give every verified fixture a documented basic screen; deepen every surviving candidate symmetrically; review material near-misses and exclusions; and stop with fewer or no final selections when the evidence warrants it. Report coverage levels honestly. Do not claim all 44 received equal deep analysis, or force six picks.
