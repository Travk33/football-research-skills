# Competition context

Read the section that matches the slate. Preserve the core evidence contract in SKILL.md.

## Domestic leagues

- Start with the current competitive season and managerial regime. Early-season samples may require a cautious previous-season baseline, adjusted for roster/role changes and explicitly labeled.
- Compare recent and venue-specific attack/defense with league-wide context. A five-game run against the bottom sides is not equivalent to one against the strongest sides. Use actual opponent adjustment only if a model implements it.
- Promotion/relegation changes the strength of opposition. Lower-division goals cannot be transferred one-for-one into top-flight expected scoring.
- Check congestion, midweek European matches, rest differences and travel. Do not apply a fixed fatigue penalty without evidence of changed minutes or performance.

## Europa League, Conference League and other European cups

- Separate qualifiers, league phase and knockouts. Account for venue and aggregate state in two-legged ties; verify the current competition rules rather than remembering obsolete formats or away-goal rules.
- Cross-league averages require strength context. Dominating a small domestic league does not by itself establish superiority over a European opponent. Retain uncertainty when the weaker-covered side has little verified data.
- Check European registration, suspensions, travel, surface/venue changes and actual rotation incentives. A domestic starter may be ineligible or rested.
- Examine whether flattering European results were driven by penalties, dismissals or one extreme opponent. Preserve both normal and sensitivity summaries.

## Internationals

- Keep competitive national matches, friendlies and club form distinct. Use club data to corroborate player role/form, not as if the national team has the same system.
- Check squad withdrawals after initial call-ups, matchday exclusions, returning suspensions and selection changes under a new coach. A bookmaker listing is not a squad confirmation.
- Qualification can change the value of a draw, rotation and game state. State the actual table arithmetic; do not assume “must win” means the team will attack well or a qualified team will be weak.
- Do not turn three or four national games into a stable long-run probability. Large mismatches and red-card matches can dominate a small sample. When underlying data are sparse, a pass is often the correct analytical output.
- Resolve neutral venues and relocated home games. A nominal home-team label may not imply normal home advantage.

## Information quality

Prefer official fixtures, national federations, clubs and competition discipline/registration notices for eligibility. Use match-data providers for statistics and preserve their definitions. Local reporting can fill tactical/news gaps but should be dated and distinguished from confirmed team sheets. Reconcile contradictory publication dates, outdated predicted XIs and reused articles before relying on them.
