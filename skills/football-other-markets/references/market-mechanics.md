# Market-specific checks

Use the sections relevant to the request. Refresh the bookmaker's rules; these are decision principles, not a substitute for current terms.

## Goal totals

For each side, assess chance volume, quality and opponent concessions. Keep one provider for numeric xG comparisons. Separate open-play and set-piece/non-penalty production when the data actually supports it. Distinguish a repeatable high-volume attack from a small set of spectacular finishes.

Game state can change the distribution: a favorite may reduce intensity after leading, while a chasing opponent may expose transitions. International qualification or two-legged aggregate state can change incentives. Do not assume all such effects increase goals.

Compare the exact offered line with counts around it and the broader baseline. Avoid assuming independence between team goals without checking whether the model and context justify it. Report red-card and penalty sensitivity rather than mechanically projecting distorted totals.

For team totals, use that team's attack versus the opponent's defense; high match totals may be driven by the other team. For first-half lines, use first-half evidence instead of dividing a full-match mean by two without justification.

## Corners

Collect team corners for/against, opposition strength, venue, minutes leading/trailing where available and corner counts by game. Compare the precise threshold. A record of 6,6,6,6 supports “at least six” descriptively but gives no observed cushion above six and only four observations.

Mechanisms include wide progression, crosses stopped at source, blocked shots and pressure forcing clearances. High possession or many goals are imperfect substitutes. An early lead can change attacking intensity; a red card can reverse territory. Team corners and result are often dependent, so price them jointly if combined.

Verify corners taken versus merely awarded, extra-time treatment and Asian/two-way/three-way settlement. For a three-way over-five line, five is not a push; for an Asian over-five line, equality can refund. Confirm the actual contract rather than infer it from a shortened slip label.

## Cards

Use the exact statistic: player booked, total cards, yellow cards, booking points or team cards. Check second-yellow/red treatment, bench/staff inclusion and extra time. A foul-count model cannot be treated as a card-count model.

Referee sample should match competition and period as closely as possible. Explain limited coverage rather than claiming a fixed player boost from a large average in an unrelated league. Consider defensive transitions, dribble exposure, aerial/ground contact, tactical fouls and player role/minutes. “Derby” or “important match” is insufficient on its own.

## Handicaps and refund markets

Translate the offered line into outcomes before pricing. Examples:

- Draw-no-bet: win earns profit, draw refunds, defeat loses.
- Asian -1: a one-goal win pushes, two or more wins, draw/defeat loses.
- Three-way -1: a one-goal win belongs to the handicap-draw outcome; it does not refund a handicap-win selection.
- Asian -0.25: half the stake is on 0 and half on -0.5; a draw loses half the stake.

For a single with refunds, `P(win)*(odds-1)-P(loss)` is expected profit per unit. Quarter-line EV is the average of its two component EVs. In an accumulator, pushes/voids generally change the return, so a single headline break-even percentage may not describe every settlement outcome. Verify bookmaker rules.

## Choosing between families

Select the market with the strongest evidence for its actual event. If a team controls territory but finishes poorly, corners may fit better than a large goals line—but only if measured corner/crossing evidence supports it. If both teams concede chances but one creates little, a favorite's team total may be more coherent than BTTS. These are hypotheses to test, not automatic substitution rules.
