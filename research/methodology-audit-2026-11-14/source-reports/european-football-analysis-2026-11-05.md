## Which metrics deserve most weight?

| Priority | Evidence | How it informs this assessment |
|---|---|---|
| Highest | Repeated chance creation and prevention: xG, xGA, preferably non-penalty and 11-v-11 | Distinguishes a convincing performance from finishing luck, penalties and long numerical advantages. Full non-penalty/event-level coverage was unavailable across this slate; no claim is made that every average was adjusted. |
| Highest | Opponent strength and actual venue | A dominant domestic record against weaker opposition cannot be transferred directly into Europe. Neutral/alternative venues also weaken generic home-advantage assumptions. |
| High | Availability, registration, rotation and expected minutes | Missing strikers affects BTTS; missing central defenders/goalkeepers affects concession risk. A projected XI is not a confirmed XI. New club updates override older previews. |
| High | Home/away form with a broader baseline | Use recent 8–10-game information where available, but avoid letting four home matches replace a team's larger record. Explicitly separate domestic, qualifying and league-phase games. |
| Supporting | Shots on target, shots in the box, big chances and xG per shot | Check whether the attack repeatedly threatens. Shot volume without shot quality can overstate strength. SOT and xG overlap; they are not independent votes. |
| Supporting | Goals, BTTS, clean sheets and failed-to-score rates | Useful checks on the pattern, but historical hit rates are not tonight's probabilities. A 100% record over three European games is weak evidence of certainty. |
| Context | Red cards, penalties, set pieces, rest, travel and tactical matchup | Interpret their actual effect. A red at minute 17 matters much more than one at minute 89. Missing fixture records must not be mistaken for extra rest. |
| Price test | Same-market odds, margin removal and uncertainty | Being likely to win is different from being worth the price. Bookmaker-derived prediction sites do not add independent confirmation. |

The preference for underlying chance quality has research support, but xG is not infallible: an empirical study found advantages over goals or shots in several prediction tasks, with performance varying by league and model. [PLOS ONE research](https://www.storre.stir.ac.uk/retrieve/be1670f4-5746-486b-b868-049e39551d0c/journal.pone.0282295.pdf). Here, data quality and game context take precedence over blindly trusting any xG figure.

For BTTS, the key question is whether **each team has a credible route to scoring**. A match with many expected goals concentrated in one favourite can still be a poor BTTS selection. For match outcomes, the stronger signal is sustained superiority in chance creation/prevention, adjusted qualitatively for the opposition and lineup.

## A transparent quantitative sensitivity check

To show what the available numbers can and cannot support, take the source-reported Midtjylland/Ajax last-ten xG averages detailed below. A deliberately simple, unadjusted calculation averages each attack with the opposing defence: Midtjylland (1.80 + 1.42)/2 = 1.61 expected goals; Ajax (2.24 + 1.60)/2 = 1.92.

Under independent Poisson scoring, BTTS = (1 − exp(−1.61)) × (1 − exp(−1.92)) ≈ **68.3%**, equivalent to fair odds around **1.46**. Moving both goal rates down/up by 0.25 changes the result to approximately **60.3%–74.8%**. This is an illustrative sensitivity range, **not a confidence interval or calibrated forecast**. The ±0.25 change is an assumed stress test, not an estimated statistical error.

The baseline does not account properly for league strength, home advantage, missing players, game-state dependence or model calibration. It therefore cannot prove value. Even this relatively favourable baseline falls below the 69.4% needed at 1.44. A formula does not turn uncertain inputs into reliable precision.
