## Iceland corners: why it earns a place

Iceland's four Nations League corner counts are **7, 2, 9, 7**; Bulgaria conceded **4, 6, 9, 5**. Thus Iceland reached five in three of four, while Bulgaria allowed five in three of four. These are seven distinct games, because the first meeting is shared. The away/home splits are less convincing: Iceland reached five in one of two away games and Bulgaria allowed five in one of two home games.

The reverse match produced a **9–1 Iceland corner advantage**. Iceland also took seven away to Estonia in a 0–0 with 62% possession, so the case does not depend on needing a large winning margin. Repeated wide deliveries in those matches provide a plausible route through blocks and deflections. This is a tactical inference supported by the match records, not a rule that possession guarantees corners. [Reverse meeting](https://www.fotmob.com/api/data/matchDetails?matchId=5181954), [Estonia away](https://www.fotmob.com/api/data/matchDetails?matchId=5181955).

The strongest counterexample is Iceland's **3–0 win at Luxembourg with only two corners**: early, efficient transition goals reduced the need to accumulate pressure. Today's away venue and Bulgaria's changed squad also weaken a simple repeat-of-the-reverse argument. Iceland's November call-up includes wide options Jón Dagur Þorsteinsson and Arnór Sigurðsson and left-sided Mikael Ellertsson; actual starts and roles remain unconfirmed. [Luxembourg away](https://www.fotmob.com/api/data/matchDetails?matchId=5181952), [KSÍ squad](https://www.ksi.is/um-ksi/frettir/landslid/hopur-a-karla-fyrir-leiki-gegn-bulgariu-og-luxemborg/).

## What is and is not established

The deeper work adds two conditional alternatives and gives evidence-based reasons for the exclusions. It does not establish that every market has been exhausted, that a five-fold is likely to win, or that three prior winning tickets validate a player-prop model. A model would need prospective probability records and calibration over a much larger sample before those claims were justified.

For the evening review, the decisive checks are the Scottish defensive XI, Iceland's width, Dutch attacking roles, Luxembourg/Estonia's actual attacking and defensive personnel, and Germany's selected attackers. For player builders, the exact joint quote and confirmed roles remain unresolved. Until those conditions are met, the appropriate output is the conditional shortlist above and **no promoted builder**.
